About the model

What it is

A logistic regression trained on every shot from three A-League Men regular seasons, 14,065 of them, of which 1,463 were goals. It uses distance, the angle of the goal, whether the shot was a header, and the phase of play. Nothing else.

What it cannot see

Where the defenders and the goalkeeper were, how much pressure the shooter was under, and what happened in the seconds before the shot. No public xG model explains more than a fraction of what decides a goal, and this one is no exception.

Either and Any

These average the model over the body parts and phases of play seen around that spot, so close to goal it leans towards headers from corners and further out towards foot shots in open play. Use a specific option when you know it.

Where it stops being evidence

Almost every header in the sample was taken inside 16 metres, and shots of any kind thin out past 35. Beyond those the page says so. Inside that range, the header penalty is a fixed amount wherever the shot is taken, so mid-range headers read a little high.

Post-shot xG

A second model, trained on the 4,834 shots that reached the frame. It takes the pre-shot xG and adds where in the goal the shot was heading, following the two-stage design Opta documents for xGOT.

Penalties

Not covered. They were left out of training, because a fixed situation taken from one spot would distort everything around it.
